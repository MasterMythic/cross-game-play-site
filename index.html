FIG. 01 — The problem

Every skin you buy dies where you bought it.

Title 01Locked
Title 02Locked
Title 03Locked

Players lease, they don't own.

Outfits, items and progress are bound to one title on one engine. When a game sunsets or a player moves on, what they paid for stays behind.

Studios rebuild the same thing again and again.

Every engine and platform wants its own mesh, rig and texture pipeline. Content gets re-authored over and over without adding any new gameplay.

Platforms need control, not chaos.

Opening the gates is only valuable if each destination can still decide what comes in, in what form, and under whose rules.

Our vision

A persistent digital identity — your avatar, your items, your achievements — that travels with you across games, engines and worlds, while every destination stays in charge of its own gate.

FIG. 02 — The granted system

How an avatar crosses between engines.

  1. 01
    Store

    A content database holds the user's 3D avatar model and the assets they own.

  2. 02
    Request

    An SDK sits inside each platform's own customisation module and requests the avatar through an API.

  3. 03
    Deliver

    The content delivery module fetches the model and assets and delivers them to the SDK at runtime.

  4. 04
    Customise natively

    The player edits the avatar inside each game, using that engine's own tools.

  5. 05
    Return

    New assets picked up in one game flow back to storage, so the identity keeps growing wherever the player goes.

Simplified from claim 1 of US 12,420,199 B2. Not claim language.

    FIG. 06 — The thesis

    In an AI world, IP is the last moat.

    AI has made writing code, generating art and shipping features cheap. Anything that can be built can now be rebuilt, faster and by anyone. What can't be rebuilt is a legal right.

    AI can write the code. It can't backdate a filing.
